Route One
Privacy Policy

We don't collect anything.

Last updated: 5 November 2026

Route One doesn't have accounts, doesn't have a server, and doesn't send anything about you anywhere. That's the whole policy — the rest of this page just explains it properly.

What the app does on your device

Route One is a self-contained app. Your scores, progress, and settings are stored only on your own device, using standard local storage built into iOS. None of it is transmitted, backed up to a server, or visible to us in any form.

What we collect

Nothing. Route One makes no network requests during play (the only exception is the optional in-app purchase, described below). There's no analytics SDK, no crash reporter phoning home, no advertising identifier, and no third-party tracking of any kind.

In-app purchase

The first 20 players in Route One are free. A single one-time purchase unlocks all remaining players. The purchase is handled entirely by Apple through the App Store: Apple processes your payment and tells the app whether the unlock has been bought. We never see or store your name, Apple ID, or payment details, and we don't keep any record of who has purchased.

The only time Route One talks to the network is when you choose to buy or restore the unlock, and that conversation is between your device and Apple.

Data shared with Apple

Apple's own platform may collect standard, anonymised operational data (like crash logs, if you opt in) as part of using any iOS app — this is covered by Apple's own privacy policies, not ours, and we don't receive or see it.

Children's privacy

Because Route One collects no data from anyone, it collects no data from children either. The app is safe for all ages from a privacy standpoint.

Changes to this policy

If Route One ever changes to collect data — for example, if a future version adds optional cloud sync — this page will be updated first, and the change will be reflected in the app's next version notes.
